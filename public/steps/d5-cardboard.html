The cardboard look

The home page and the studio are now cut from cardboard, like the sets kids build for their films: pieces of the cardboard letters font's own photos, with paper pasted on them. Built over many small rounds, by eye; this page sums up where it landed.

What was built

  • One set of mixins, src/styles/_cardboard.scss: cardboard($edge, $shadow) a piece (its corrugated edges, its surface, its shadow), cardboard-paper($colour) paper pasted on it up to the rim, cardboard-button a button (a shade under the pointer, pressed down), cardboard-split a button joined to its options arrow. The colours moved to _colours.scss.
  • Home page: the title in paper letters on a five-piece burst, the buttons and the animation cards, shown frame by frame as a short stop-motion intro (not when coming back from the studio, not with reduced motion). The storage meter is in a Settings window. The cardboard pictures come from the font project: npm run sync:cardboard (README).
  • Studio:
    • the back arrow, Save and Help in a column beside the stage, one size;
    • Play and See under the stage, each one piece with its options arrow;
    • the stage on cardboard, with a white paper border;
    • the side panel and its tabs, reaching down to the timeline;
    • the timeline as bare cardboard from edge to edge, its tools a tab cut at 45°;
    • the playhead and the selection as one red block behind the selected pictures, with a chunky white grip; the ruler's gradations a setting in the Layers menu, off at first;
    • the extra rows with dashed dividers and their names ("Objects", "Sounds").
  • Design system page: a Cardboard section after the tokens (the pieces, the buttons beside the stage, Play and See). The top toolbar and plain split-button specimens are gone, and two tokens nothing used any more (--shadow-card, --color-faint).
  • Along the way: a new animation always opens on the Camera tab; the GIF's size is a slider; the timeline zooms in up to 16 times (2560 px a second).

Decisions

  • Square, no easing. Cardboard is cut straight; hover and press jump from one pose to the next, as in stop motion.
  • White paper darker under the pointer, coloured paper lighter: lighter white hardly shows.
  • Box shadows on pieces that hold things fixed to the window (the timeline, the side panel): a filter shadow would make a dragged picture follow the piece. A thin dark ring on the piece's last pixel hides the edge photo's pale highlight, which read as paper under the cardboard.

Check it

  • Open the home page: the intro plays once; come back from the studio: it doesn't.
  • In the studio: the pieces don't move under the pointer, only their paper changes; select several pictures: one red block behind each, the grip on the last.
  • /design: the Cardboard section.
Checked while building: each round by screenshot and the specs of the flows it touched; design.spec covers the page, frames, playback and timeline specs the playhead block, the grip and the gradations.
