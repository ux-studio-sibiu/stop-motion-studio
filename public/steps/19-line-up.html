Line up with other pictures

Step 18 made a picture movable. This step makes it easy to put it exactly where it belongs:

  • Spot the difference, a see-through look beside "Outline only": the picture before is laid over the current one so that everything that lines up goes black, and what doesn't glows. It works over the live camera too.
  • With the camera off and the Picture tab open, a drag snaps the picture's edges and centre to the stage's and to the see-through picture's, with a guide line.
  • Ctrl + wheel (or a trackpad pinch) zooms the stage to look closer, and Space-drag looks around.
Simplified in the same step. The first version had its own "Line up with" section in the Picture tab, with:
  • a ghost of the picture before or after;
  • a zoom slider with Fit and 100%;
  • "copy position" buttons.
The see-through dropdown already shows the picture before, so that section went. "Spot the difference" became a see-through option, and zoom stayed on the stage itself.

What was built

  • src/core/model/snap.ts: snapBox, boxLines, boundsOf. 5 tests.
  • In the renderer:
    • drawOnionSkin takes difference: only the nearest picture, at full strength, never in the outline look. 1 pixel test.
    • frameCorners: a picture's corners on the canvas, for the outline and for snapping. 1 test.
  • src/core/render/stageView.ts: the view zoom and pan (clampView, zoomView, panView). 4 tests.
  • In the store:
    • onionSkin.difference, with one look at a time (outline or difference);
    • stageView, which goes back to fit when another animation opens.
    Tests changed for both.
  • OnionSkin.tsx marks the canvas .is-difference. CSS blends it with what's under it (mix-blend-mode: difference), whether that's the camera or a picture.
  • In the see-through dropdown, the "Spot the difference" checkbox. While it's on, "How strong" and "Pictures shown" are greyed out.
  • StageView.tsx: the stage's layers, zoomed and panned as one by a CSS transform.
  • StageEditor.tsx: snapping with guides, and Alt to place freely.
  • useStagePan.ts, used by the stage editor: Space-drag or the middle button to pan, and Ctrl + wheel to zoom around the pointer.
  • Each animation's zoom and pan are remembered (remembered.ts). They're also saved when the page is left, so a quick reload doesn't lose them.
  • 3 end-to-end tests: spot the difference in stage.spec.ts; snapping and zoom in transform.spec.ts.

Decisions

  • One see-through, several looks. Photo, outline only, or spot the difference: one ghost, one place to set it. The difference is a CSS blend, so it works over the live <video> as well as over a picture. It's only a looking aid: export never draws it.
  • Only drags snap. Arrow keys and typed numbers move exactly what they say. The snap distance is 8 screen pixels at any zoom, and Alt turns snapping off.
  • Two zooms never mix (PLAN.md §1). The view zoom is a CSS transform on the stage's layers, only while the Picture tab is open on a picture; the picture's size is part of its transform. The pointer maths measures the layer on screen, so a nudge is still one project pixel at any zoom.

Not in this step

  • Lining up with the picture after, or with any chosen picture: see-through shows the ones before.
  • Copying a position to other pictures. It was built, then taken out to keep the Picture tab simple. The command (setTransforms) is there if it's wanted back.
  • On-screen zoom buttons, and two-finger pan and pinch on touch screens. A trackpad pinch works, because it arrives as Ctrl + wheel.

Watch out

  • The see-through picture is drawn with its own transform, never the shown picture's (pixel test).
  • Snap guides only show during a drag, and the snap follows the first picture's box, so several selected pictures move together.
  • Saves that wait a moment ("debounced") can be lost on reload. The stage view's save is flushed on pagehide; the end-to-end test that pans and reloads straight away found this.

Check it

  • Shift a picture by hand, tick "Spot the difference", nudge until the background goes black.
  • Zoom in with Ctrl + wheel and nudge: the picture moves one video pixel. Reload: the zoom is still there.
