Build steps · Step 10 of 33 · Part 4 Timeline

Time-scaled timeline and seconds ruler

The timeline is now drawn to time. A picture is exactly as wide as it lasts on screen, and a faint ruler above counts seconds. Zoom in and out with the buttons or Ctrl + mouse wheel. Click a picture to select it, then move with the arrow keys. This is the base for resizing (step 13), reordering (14) and the playhead (16).

What was built

  • src/core/model/timing.ts: ticksToSeconds, secondsToTicks, pxPerTick, frameStarts, totalTicks, frameIndexAt (which picture is on screen at a tick), rulerScale and formatSeconds. 18 tests.
  • src/studio/Timeline.tsx, rewritten:
    • tools: zoom out, zoom in, the animation's length;
    • the seconds ruler;
    • the picture track: each picture hold × px per tick wide;
    • keyboard selection;
    • Ctrl/Cmd + wheel zoom around the pointer;
    • auto-scroll to the selection and to new pictures.
  • Default zoom 160 px per second: at the default 8 pictures per second, one tick is 20 px. The timeline area is taller (10.5 rem) to fit the tools, the ruler and a track you can tap.
  • .icon-button.is-small for the zoom buttons (still 40 px).
  • e2e/timeline.spec.ts (5 tests). The page object now finds pictures as listbox options, and checks thumbnails through their background image.

The numbers

px per tick   = px per second / fps          160 / 8 = 20 px
picture width = hold × px per tick           a 3-tick picture = 60 px
starts        = [0, hold₀, hold₀ + hold₁, …] where each picture begins, in ticks
length        = total ticks / fps            24 ticks at 8 fps = 3.0 s

Decisions

Ticks underneath, seconds and pixels only for showing

All the maths starts from ticks (step 02): frameStarts puts pictures end to end, frameIndexAt finds the picture under a tick with a binary search (the playhead will use it), and seconds and pixels are derived from ticks when drawing. Changing the speed later rescales the whole timeline, ruler included, without touching a single picture.

Widths are exact, so the ruler lines up

A gap between pictures would push every picture a few pixels off its time. Instead pictures sit edge to edge, separated by a 1 px line drawn inside each one (an inset box-shadow), and the selection outline is drawn inside too. The fourth picture starts exactly where the ruler says it does.

The ruler is a background, not hundreds of elements

The marks are one repeating gradient sized to the mark spacing (background-size: markSeconds × zoom); only the labels are elements. rulerScale decides the spacing:

  • Labels: every 1, 2, 5, 10, 15, 30… seconds, the first that leaves at least 64 px between labels.
  • Marks: every second, and every half or quarter second once zoomed in far enough to tell them apart.

The ruler is faint (line colour, muted small text) so the pictures stay the main thing.

Thumbnails as a repeating background

A picture's thumbnail is its background, centred, at the track's height, repeating sideways. A short picture shows the middle of its thumbnail (cover-cropped); a long one shows it two or three times, which reads as "this picture stays a while". One CSS rule does both.

A listbox, for keyboards and screen readers

The track is role="listbox" and each picture is an option named "Picture 3" with aria-selected. The listbox gets keyboard focus (Tab), and the selected picture is announced through aria-activedescendant. Arrows move the selection, Home and End jump to the ends, and Escape clears it. Clicking a picture focuses the track, so the arrow keys work straight away. Selecting several pictures comes with the frame operations (step 12).

Zoom keeps your place

Zooming changes how many pixels a second gets, so everything moves. To stay oriented, the time under the pointer (Ctrl + wheel), or under the selected picture (buttons), or the middle of the view, is remembered before the zoom and scrolled back under the same spot right after it (in a layout effect, before the browser paints). Zoom goes from 40 to 640 px per second, in steps of 1.5×. It's how you look at the timeline, not part of the animation, so it's kept in the store and not saved.

Ctrl + wheel needs a native listener

React attaches wheel handlers as "passive", which means they can't call preventDefault(), so Ctrl + wheel would zoom the whole page instead. The timeline adds its own wheel listener with { passive: false } and only takes over when Ctrl (or Cmd) is held; plain wheel and trackpad swipes still scroll.

Watch out

  • Narrow pictures at high speed. At 15 pictures per second and the default zoom, a one-tick picture is about 11 px wide. Widths are never padded (the ruler must line up), so zoom in to pick out single pictures; picture numbers only show when a picture is at least 24 px wide. The track is 56 px tall, so it's still easy to hit.
  • Thumbnails aren't <img> any more. Tests that check a thumbnail read the element's background-image URL (see Studio.thumbnailOf() in e2e/fixtures.ts).
  • The ruler is drawn a little past the end (2 s), under the "next picture" slot, so there's always time to the right of the last picture.
  • Long animations are a wide element with many pictures; virtualising it (drawing only what's visible) is step 31.

npm run dev
  • Take a dozen pictures: each is a narrow slice (20 px); the ruler shows 0s, 1s, 2s, with 8 pictures per second; the length reads "1.5 s".
