Frame operations

Pictures can now be duplicated, several can be selected at once, and a new picture goes right after the selected one instead of always at the end. A child can go back to picture 3, take two more there, copy a picture to make a pause, or select five and delete them together. Every one of these is a command, so undo works for all of them.

What was built

  • cloneFrame(frame) in core/model/project.ts: an exact copy with a new id. 2 tests.
  • core/model/selection.ts: selectRange, toggleInSelection, inOrder, insertIndexAfter. 6 tests.
  • duplicateFrames(projectId, sources, order) in core/commands; addFrames now selects what it added.
  • FrameInsert.after: insert right after a given frame, looked up inside the transaction. 1 contract test.
  • The timeline:
    • Shift+click, Ctrl/Cmd+click, Shift+arrows and Ctrl/Cmd+A to select;
    • Duplicate (button and Ctrl/Cmd+D) next to Delete;
    • a red marker after the selected picture showing where the next capture goes.
  • useCapture inserts after the rightmost selected picture.
  • e2e/frames.spec.ts (6 tests).

Decisions

A duplicate looks exactly like its source

cloneFrame keeps everything but the id:

  • the same imageId (the photo is shared, not copied);
  • the same hold;
  • copies of transform and adjust.

Sharing the photo makes duplicating free: the storage meter doesn't move, and a test checks the image count stays the same. A frame being moved, zoomed or brightened (steps 18–19) copies exactly too. One test sets those values in storage, duplicates, and compares the copy with its source field by field.

Nested objects are copied, never shared: otherwise moving the copy would move the source. A test walks every field of a frame and fails if any object or array is the same one in both. When speech bubbles arrive (step 23, elements), forgetting to copy them fails that test straight away.

Each copy goes right after its own source

Selecting a and b and duplicating gives a a' b b', not a b a' b'. In stop motion, a copy right after its picture is how you hold a pose for longer. The copies are selected afterwards, so duplicating again keeps stretching.

The copies are placed with restoreFrames (step 11), which puts frames at given final positions in one transaction. Undo removes them and selects the sources; redo puts the very same copies back.

The next picture goes after the selection

A capture goes right after the rightmost selected picture, or at the end when nothing is selected. It then selects itself, so the next capture follows it: taking five pictures in a row after picture 3 keeps them in order. A thin capture-red bar on the selected picture's right edge shows where the next one will land; it's hidden when that is simply the end, next to the camera slot. Escape clears the selection, so the next picture goes at the end again.

Fast captures stay in order

The capture selects itself only once storage reports it back (storage is the truth, step 07). A second capture before that report would still see the old selection, and land before the first one. Two things prevent that:

  • An id, not an index. The insert names the frame to follow (after), and the repository looks it up inside the transaction, where the order is always current.
  • Remember the last capture. useCapture remembers the frame it just added. If the selection hasn't changed since then, the next picture goes after that frame.

Selecting several pictures

  • Click selects one picture.
  • Shift + click selects everything from the anchor (where the range started) to here.
  • Ctrl/Cmd + click adds a picture to the selection, or takes it out.
  • Shift + ← → grows or shrinks the range from the keyboard.
  • Ctrl/Cmd + A selects all.

The selection is always kept in timeline order. The "focus" (the end that moves, and what a screen reader announces) is the timeline's own state. When a capture or an undo changes the selection, the focus falls back to its end. The listbox says aria-multiselectable. The Delete and Duplicate buttons name the count: "Delete 3 pictures".

Flaky tests, found and fixed

With 60 tests, running Chromium and WebKit together made the machine busy enough for two timing assumptions to break now and then:

  • Camera start. The fake camera sometimes took more than Playwright's default 5 seconds to start. Studio.cameraOn() now allows 15 seconds for the capture button.
  • The late-first-frame camera test (step 09). It used a 1.5-second timer, which a busy machine could reach before the test even looked at the button. Now the test itself sends the first frame (window.sendFirstFrame()), after checking that the button is off and stays off. It no longer depends on timing, and it still fails without the step 09 fix.

Chromium alone was stable throughout; afterwards the full suite passed three times in a row.

Watch out

  • Selecting several on a tablet needs Shift or Ctrl, which a touch screen doesn't have. A "select" mode for touch belongs with the kids' polish (Part 10); for now a child on a tablet works one picture at a time.
  • A capture now selects itself. Tests that expect nothing to be selected after taking pictures must press Escape first (history.spec.ts changed for that reason).
  • Every new Frame field must be handled in cloneFrame(). A nested one must be copied, and the test above will remind you.
  • Undoing a capture leaves nothing selected, so the next picture goes at the end.
