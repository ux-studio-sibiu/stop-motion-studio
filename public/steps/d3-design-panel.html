The floating tweak panel

On /design, a panel floats over the gallery with every design token in its groups. Change one and everything on the page follows at once.

  • A control for each kind: colours get a picker and their hex (or a text field, for colours with transparency); lengths get a slider, a number and their unit; shadows and the font, a text field.
  • Light / dark: which theme is shown and tuned, whatever the system's is. Each keeps its own tweaks.
  • Filter by name or by what the token is for. Changed tokens are marked, with a count, and each has its reset.
  • Dragged by its bar; Shift+T (or the Tokens button) closes and opens it.
  • Pick: click an element on the page (it's outlined under the pointer; Esc stops) and the list shows only the tokens it uses, with a "hover, focus" switch for its other looks. A click on an icon picks the button or element holding it. × shows all tokens again.
  • Hovering a token in the list outlines every element on the page that uses it.

Decisions

  • Its own shadow root: the panel's styles are its own, and it never uses the app's tokens, so tuning them can't restyle it.
  • On the page's root, while the page is open: the tokens are set as custom properties on the document, so the page background follows too; they're taken off when the page goes, so the studio never sees them. Nothing is written anywhere yet (D4 keeps them).
  • The kits' look, written small: black and white, mono labels, a hard shadow, like the type and texture kits in the other repos. Not copied from their shell: that needs the kits' other stylesheets and a few hundred lines of icons, more than this page needs.
  • The gallery's token cards show the values from the panel's state, so they change as you tune.
  • How Pick finds the tokens (tokenScan.ts): the browser only gives final values, so it reads the page's stylesheets. It takes every rule that applies to the element (its ::before and ::after, and with the switch its hover, focus and pressed looks), its inline style, and what it inherits from its parents (text colour, font, sizes), collects the var(--…) in them, and follows a component's own variables (the tabs' --edge) back to the tokens. Things drawn on a canvas use their own settings, not tokens, so they show none.
