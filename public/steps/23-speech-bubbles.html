Objects on pictures: speech bubbles

A dinosaur can talk now. The new Objects tab puts things on a picture, with the camera off. A speech bubble is the first kind of object; more will come.

  • Adding: the tab shows the kinds of object as icons. Drag one onto the picture on the stage and it lands where it's dropped. A click, or the keyboard, puts it near the top instead. A new bubble is picked straight away, with the keyboard in its text.
  • Settings, in the tab: below the icons, in a card of its own, the picked object's settings. For a bubble, in two groups:
    • Bubble: its shape from a dropdown (speech, thought or spiked), a Tail checkbox, its colour, its outline (none, thin, medium or thick) and the outline's colour;
    • Text: the text, shown on the picture as it's typed; its size, font and colour.
  • Placing, on the stage: the same for every kind of object, with nothing for it in the tab.
    • Click an object to pick it, then drag it to move it.
    • Its corners size it, the round handle turns it, and a bubble's dot moves its tail.
    • Delete removes it, the arrow keys nudge it, and Enter or a double-click goes to a bubble's text.

Each change is one undo step. An object belongs to its picture: move the picture or copy it, and the object goes along. The timeline thumbnails show a picture's objects, and its move, size, turn and flip, as they change. See-through shows the previous pictures' objects, so they can be lined up too.

What was built

  • The model, src/core/model/element.ts:
    • the Element type (an object: a kind, a box, its own settings), and Frame.elements;
    • the speech bubble's settings, BubbleProps: shape, tail and whether it shows, text, text size, font, text colour, fill, outline and outline colour; bubbleProps reads what's stored defensively, with defaults;
    • BUBBLE_FONTS: Nunito (the app's, self-hosted) in two weights, and Comic, Classic and Typewriter from fonts most computers have, each with a generic fallback;
    • the box maths: moveBox, resizeBox (from a corner, also when turned), rotateBox, boxCorners, boxAt (where a drop lands), and pointInBox / boxPoint (the tail, in the box's own terms and back);
    • cloneElements and sameElements. createFrame and cloneFrame handle elements: a copied picture's objects are its own.
  • One command, setElements(projectId, frameId, from, to, label) in core/commands, for any change to a picture's objects.
  • Old frames: frames saved before this step read as having no objects, through a Dexie reading hook in db.ts; no migration.
  • Drawing, in src/core/render/:
    • text.ts: wrapText (canvas text doesn't wrap) and fitText (the largest size that fits, down to a readable minimum);
    • bubble.ts: speech, thought and spiked bubbles, with or without their tail, in their colours, outline and font;
    • elements.ts: a plain map from kind to drawing function. renderAt draws a picture's objects on top of it, so the stage, playback and export match.
    • drawOnionSkin includes a ghost picture's objects, drawn whole on a scratch canvas (scratch.ts, shared with the brightness fallback), then laid down faintly in one go;
    • transformCss: a picture's move, size, turn and flip as a CSS transform, for the thumbnails.
  • The store: an elements preview (a fourth entry in the shared preview table) and selectedElement. useShownFrames includes the preview.
  • The Objects tab, src/features/objects/:
    • ObjectsPanel: the icons, then the picked object's settings card, placed by kind;
    • ObjectTile: an icon dragged onto the stage with pointer events (a finger works too), an icon following the pointer and the stage lit up while it's over it;
    • BubbleSettings: a bubble's settings;
    • ObjectEditor: the stage layer, the same box editing for every kind;
    • objectFrame: the picture being edited, and the shared show-then-save.
    Studio.tsx places the tab and its stage layer directly, like the others. useStudioCommands gains setObjects.
  • Thumbnails, src/studio/timeline/Thumb.tsx: the photo fitted like the stage fits it, moved with transformCss, brightness and contrast on the photo only, and the objects drawn on a small canvas on top. The timeline uses changes not saved yet, so a thumbnail follows a drag or typing as it happens. The thumbnail sits inside the picture's dotted outline. A mark on thumbnails of pictures with objects.
  • The canvas draws again once web fonts have loaded (useCanvasDraw), so text never keeps a fallback font.

Decisions

  • Objects belong to their picture. They live on the frame, so reordering, duplicating and deleting a picture take its objects along with no extra code. The box is in fractions of the frame, and isn't moved by the picture's own position or size.
  • Settings in the tab, placement on the stage. What an object is (shape, text, colours) is chosen in the tab, per kind. Where it is, how big and how it's turned is done by hand on the stage, the same for every kind, with no number fields.
  • Drag and drop to add, with pointer events rather than HTML drag and drop, so it works on a tablet. A click still adds, so the keyboard can too.
  • One command, the whole list. setElements saves a picture's objects from what they were to what they become, so adding, typing, moving, restyling and deleting are each one undo step. A run of typing or nudges is one step (saveSoon).
  • Typing in the tab. The text box is a normal <textarea> in the settings card, so typing, selecting and the on-screen keyboard work as usual; the bubble on the picture follows as it's typed.
  • Readable at GIF size. The text size is the largest the text gets; it shrinks to fit the bubble, but never below 3.5% of the frame's height, so a 360 px tall GIF still gets 13 px text.
  • Old bubbles look as before. Settings added later are read with defaults: a tail, a medium dark outline, white, Nunito.
  • Kinds are a plain map (elements.ts), not a registry. A new kind adds a drawing function there, a tile and a settings component in the tab. An unknown kind is skipped, never an error.
  • Thumbnails use CSS, not the renderer. The small saved photo, moved with a CSS transform (tested to land where the renderer puts it), with only the objects drawn: cheap for hundreds of pictures. Only a thumbnail whose objects changed redraws.

Watch out

  • Enter is Play's shortcut. It used to start playback even when a stage editing layer had the keyboard. ownsKey (keyboard.ts) now leaves Space and Enter to anything with role="application", which both stage editing layers have.
  • See-through is drawn over the stage, objects included, so with see-through on they look tinted by the previous picture. That's how see-through already worked.
  • Fonts: Nunito is self-hosted from public/fonts/nunito/ (src/styles/_fonts.scss), with the files our own build had downloaded, so there are no requests to Google. The SIL Open Font License text still needs adding there as OFL.txt before the app is published. Comic, Classic and Typewriter use fonts already on the computer, so they can look a little different where those are missing.
  • Arrow keys: with the keyboard on the stage layer, they nudge the picked object, not the timeline's selection.
  • Thumbnails: text is too small to read there, and the saved thumbnail photo is low resolution, so a heavily zoomed picture looks blurry in it.

Check it

  • Drag a bubble onto picture 3, type, then move picture 3 to the start (Ctrl+←): the bubble goes with it, and its thumbnail shows it. Duplicate it: the copy has one too.
  • Make it spiked, without a tail, with a thick blue outline; undo once: only the outline colour goes back.
  • Turn see-through on and pick picture 4: picture 3's bubble shows faintly.
  • Reload: everything is still there.
