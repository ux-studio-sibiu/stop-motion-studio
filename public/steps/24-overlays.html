Objects in time: the overlay track, text and shapes

A title can now stay up for two seconds, whatever pictures are under it. There's a second track under the pictures for objects that live in time, and two new kinds of object: Text and a configurable Shape.

  • Putting an object in time: drag an Objects tab icon onto the track under the pictures. It shows from where it was dropped, for 2 seconds to start with, over every picture then. Dropped on the picture instead, an object belongs to that picture, as before.
  • On the track: each object in time is a bar from when it starts to when it ends.
    • Drag the bar to move it in time, and its ends to change when it starts or ends.
    • Click it to pick it. It becomes the timeline's selection instead of the pictures, so the toolbar's Duplicate and Delete act on it. The stage goes to the picture where it starts, which gets a quiet dashed ring (on the stage, not selected), and the Objects tab shows its settings.
    • Duplicate puts the copy right after it. Clicking a picture makes the pictures the selection again; Shift- and Ctrl-click still select several pictures.
    • With the keyboard on a bar: ← → move it, Shift with them makes it shorter or longer, Ctrl+D duplicates it, and Delete removes it.
    • The picked bar is remembered after a reload, like selected pictures.
    Overlapping bars go in rows, so none hides another.
  • On the stage it's placed like any object (move, size, turn), and its settings are in the Objects tab, which also says when it shows.
  • Text: text on its own, with its size, font and colour, and a background colour or none.
  • Shape: one kind of object whose shape is a setting: rectangle, ellipse or arrow. It has a colour or none, and an outline. Turn an arrow on the stage to point it anywhere.

Every change is one undo step. Moving, resizing, reordering or deleting pictures never moves an object in time, and changing the speed keeps it over the same pictures.

Decisions

  • Drop on the track to put an object in time. It's the same drag and drop as putting an object on a picture, aimed at a different place, so no extra switch or mode is needed.
  • Overlays live on the project record, not in a new table as the roadmap first said. They're a short list, saved and watched with the project like its speed or order, so there's no migration and no second watch. Projects from before read as having none.
  • One command, the whole list, like a picture's objects. Adding, moving, resizing, restyling and deleting are each one undo step, and undoing a picture's delete never touches overlays.
  • One Shape kind, its form a setting (your call), instead of separate rectangle, ellipse and arrow kinds: a rectangle can become an arrow without starting again.
  • Anchored to time. start and length are ticks, like a picture's hold. Pictures moving don't move overlays, and changing the speed keeps them over the same pictures.
  • One timeline selection. Pictures or one bar: what the toolbar acts on is never in doubt. The picture under a picked bar stays chosen for the stage, shown as on the stage (aria-current), not selected (aria-selected).
  • Rows only for drawing. Overlapping overlays are drawn in rows (overlayLanes) but are still one track; on the stage they stack by start time, the later on top. More tracks are a track number away.
  • The stage edits what shows at the picture on the stage, at the time it starts. Picking a bar takes the stage there.

Watch out

  • Grips: the overlay track is its own row, with its own 8 px ends to grab, so they don't get in the way of the pictures' resize grips.
  • Past the last picture: a bar can run past the end of the pictures, and the track shows it. Playback still stops at the last picture. Export (step 25) should run to the later of the two.
  • See-through and thumbnails leave overlays out. A ghost or a thumbnail is one picture, and an overlay doesn't belong to a picture. Drawing overlays there would show them twice on the stage.
  • A picture looked at on its own shows every overlay that overlaps it. At first the stage drew a picture at the moment it starts, so a bar starting partway through wasn't there to see or edit. Now the stage (not playing) uses renderPicture, which draws every overlay showing at any moment of the picture (overlaysDuring). Playback and export (renderAt) still show an overlay only from the moment it starts. Found with long pictures; the specs had used one-tick pictures.
  • The picked object's settings stay in the Objects tab whatever picture the stage shows, even when it isn't on that picture (it then says so: "Not on this picture: it shows from … to …"). useStageObjects gives the picked object wherever it lives.
  • Strict test queries: the new Shape tile is named "Shape", like the bubble's Shape dropdown, so the specs ask for the dropdown by role (combobox).

Check it

  • Drop a Text on the track at 0 s and type a title. It shows over the first 2 seconds of pictures. Change the speed: it still covers the same pictures.
  • Drag pictures around and delete one: the title stays at 0–2 s.
  • Drag the bar's end to make it longer; undo; drag the bar later; Delete it; undo.
  • Add a Shape, make it an arrow and turn it on the stage; set its colour to None.
